## About This Project

In the reference book, every chapter and appendix closes with some homework problems, I tried to solve them
here. Proofs are written out in full; numerical problems are solved in R, with the book's replication data
([Ding, 2023](#ref-ding-2023)), in the `causalding` package that lives alongside this site, and their results
are drawn with D3.js.

There are also some notes and explanations for some things I found worth highlighting, if nothing else,
because it helps me understand the material better, and serve as a reference for future use.

## How the Figures Are Made

Numbers behind the solutions are done in R, in the `causalding` package, and most figures are exported
directly from R. When interacting with a figure adds a layer of understanding, for example by varying a
parameter and watching the result respond, the figure is drawn with D3.js instead, from data computed in R.

The figure below is an example of such cases, it extends Section 1.4 of the book, the admissions to the six
largest departments of the graduate school at Berkeley ([Bickel et al., 1975](#ref-bickel-1975)) with gender
as the treatment and admission as the outcome, and draws it in the geometry of Section 1.3.3.

<figure className="doc-figure">

<YuleSimpsonExplorer />

<figcaption>

Each department is a vector whose run is its rejected applicants and whose rise is its admitted ones, so its
slope is the odds of admission and a steeper vector means a higher admission rate. The vectors of a group are
laid tip to tail: solid for male applicants ($Z = 1$), dashed for female applicants ($Z = 0$). The straight
line from the origin to the end of a path has the slope $n_{z1}/n_{z0}$ of the aggregated table. Point at a
department, or tab to it, to lay its two vectors side by side at the origin: in four of the six departments
the dashed one is the steeper, yet the dashed chord is the flatter.

**Aggregated** gives every department the aggregated rate of its group. Each path straightens onto its chord
and its end does not move, because the aggregated rate is the average of the department rates weighted by
$\mathrm{pr}(D = d \mid Z = z)$. The slider moves these weights for women a fraction $t$ of the way to the
men's while keeping each department's rates, and the lower panel, which can also be dragged, follows
$\mathrm{rd}$ as it changes sign at $t^*$. At $t = 1$ it is an average of the differences within departments,
$\mathrm{rd} = \sum_d \mathrm{pr}(D = d \mid Z = 1)\{\mathrm{pr}(Y = 1 \mid Z = 1, D = d) - \mathrm{pr}(Y = 1 \mid Z = 0, D = d)\}$.
The reweighting is arithmetic on the observed rates, not an effect of gender: applicants choose a department
after their gender is fixed, a situation Part VI of the book returns to.

</figcaption>

</figure>
